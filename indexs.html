There was nothing
to start from.

Freedom Broker Kyrgyzstan is a licensed broker inside Freedom Holding Corp. (NASDAQ: FRHC), running brokerage, dealer, trust management and virtual-asset exchange services.

When I joined as Head of Academy there was no learning function. No platform. No course library. No onboarding path. No record of who had been trained on what. Compliance training was a regulatory obligation with nothing behind it.

In four months: a platform chosen and implemented, a course library, a company-wide compliance programme and a five-week onboarding path.

The platform

I chose the LMS.
Then I built everything in it.

The company had no learning platform at all. I ran the selection and the rollout end to end and landed on Evolve, an AI-native LMS with SCORM support, native authoring and an assessment centre. It went live and the whole course library was built on it from nothing.

Built in-house

SCORM packages produced with AI tooling and Figma rather than bought or briefed out. Turnaround on a new mandatory course went from a procurement cycle to a production task.

Formal standing

I wrote the Academy's governing document, thirteen chapters covering structure, functions, attestation procedure, KPI and reporting. Without that, a learning function is a service desk.

Inside a course

Thirty-seven screens
on a US accounting law.

A mandatory SOX course for every employee of a NASDAQ-listed broker. The hard part is not the law. It is making a compliance obligation feel like something that has to do with the person reading it. Designed and produced in-house.

The rules, up front. Who it is for, the pass mark, how many attempts. Nobody should meet the assessment rules for the first time at the test.

Contents and outcomes on one screen. Eight blocks on the left, what you will be able to do afterwards on the right. Written as capabilities, not topics.

Three framings, side by side. What this is, why the company needs it, why it matters to you. Compliance training fails when it never answers the third one.

Practice checks that do not count. Retrieval between blocks, explicitly ungraded. Scoring everything teaches people to guess safely rather than think.

Compliance programme

Eleven courses.
One at a time.

The usual approach is to release everything at once and chase people until the deadline. I sequenced them instead: two to three working days per course, weekends excluded, a fixed close date for each, spread across twenty-five working days.

Staggering removes the collision between training and the working day. Non-completion shows up on day three rather than at the deadline. And compliance gets a defensible record of when each obligation was met, course by course.

Onboarding programme

Fifty-one items.
Every one with an owner.

Nothing in the programme says "read this". Each line specifies what the person must be able to do, who checks it, and on which day. Blended across courses, self-study, briefings, meetings and mandatory practice with a mentor on a demo account.

WEEK 1

Contracts, equipment, platform and CRM access, the holding and the local entity, regulation and investor protection, role KPI and compensation, code of ethics.

WEEK 2

Market structure, world exchanges, equities, bonds, ETFs, IPO, options, investment risk, finance fundamentals, technical and fundamental analysis.

WEEK 3

Brokerage account, savings sub-account, trust management, tariffs, platform navigation, opening a client account end to end, demo portfolios, order types, security.

WEEK 4

Tone of voice, call structure, five inbound and outbound scripts, complaint handling, response standards, needs discovery, objections, cross-sell and closing, prohibited phrases.

WEEK 5

Risk disclaimer, personal data and client verification, anti-fraud, IT security, mandatory group compliance courses, satisfaction survey, individual development plan.

Rising thresholds

Six checkpoints instead of one exam at the end. 80% on day ten, 80% on day fifteen, 85% on day twenty, then a three-scenario role-play, a mentor sign-off on supervised live calls, and a final joint approval.

Seven owners

Employee, mentor, department head, L&D, Compliance, IT and HR each have defined responsibilities in the document. A programme that only L&D owns is one that quietly stops running the moment L&D is busy.
